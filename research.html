Wind Integration and Short-Run Market Power in Wholesale Electricity Markets: Evidence from ERCOT

Paper (PDF) ↗

Abstract

Renewable integration changes the competitive environment in wholesale electricity markets, potentially altering conventional generators' incentives to exercise market power. Using generator-level bidding and operating data from ERCOT, this paper examines how wind generation affects market power and welfare. I develop a supply-side model of strategic bidding that distinguishes static market power from dynamic operating considerations. Empirical results show that a 1 GW increase in expected wind generation is associated with a $1.38/MWh reduction in bid markups, approximately 30 percent of the average markup. A model-based decomposition identifies changes in residual demand as the primary mechanism. Using the calibrated model, counterfactual simulations show that a 1 GW increase in wind capacity raises annual consumer surplus by $1.30 billion while reducing producer surplus by $1.01 billion, with endogenous strategic bidding amplifying these distributional effects by approximately 10 percent. However, static market power limits total welfare gains by 54 percent relative to a benchmark without static market power.
